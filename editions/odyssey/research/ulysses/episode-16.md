# Episode 16 — “Eumaeus”

**Scope.** “Eumaeus” is a title from Joyce’s private schemas, retained here as a research label; interpretive comparisons remain provisional. Locators use `sources/ulysses-16.txt`, with global line numbers cross-referenced by `sources/ulysses-boundaries.json`.

## Story and technique

[T] After Nighttown, Bloom helps the shaken Stephen and proposes the cabman’s shelter. There they encounter the sailor Murphy and hear stories of travel, ships, Gibraltar, and a son who has gone to sea. Nationalist talk about Ireland follows. Bloom shows Stephen a photograph of Molly, imagines introducing them, and tries to become a practical protector. They leave the shelter and walk together toward Bloom’s house; Stephen remains wary and does not stay overnight.

[T] The prose is deliberately tired, indirect, and repetitious. [I] Its awkwardness is not merely a joke about bad writing: the style records conversation after exhaustion, when people reach for stock phrases because exact intimacy is difficult. Hospitality becomes an effort made through imperfect language.

## Important passages and close reading

[T] The sailor’s tales are difficult to verify and shift between autobiography, boast, and borrowed adventure. [I] His unreliability matters because the episode is about stories that promise a world beyond the city while revealing how little one person can know of another.

[T] Bloom’s practical care—finding drink, protecting Stephen, suggesting Molly’s musical welcome—contrasts with Stephen’s resistance to being absorbed into a paternal story. [I] The possibility of a father-son relation is offered as hospitality, not completed as a fact. The shelter protects them temporarily without creating a new household.

## Homeric echo and crucial difference

[C] “Eumaeus” recalls Odysseus’ loyal swineherd, whose shelter and recognition help restore the household. The cabman’s shelter is a modern refuge, and its sailor supplies a wandering counterpart to the returned hero.

[I] There is no reliable recognition, loyal retainer, or completed return. Bloom’s hospitality is generous but awkward; Stephen’s acceptance is partial. The echo measures the distance between epic restoration and modern provisional companionship.

## Reading question

Is hospitality here a form of fatherhood, a temporary rescue, or simply two exhausted people sharing a route home?
