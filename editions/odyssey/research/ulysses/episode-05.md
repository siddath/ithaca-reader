# Episode 5 — “Lotus Eaters”

**Scope.** “Lotus Eaters” is a title from Joyce’s private schemas, retained here as a research label; interpretive comparisons remain provisional. Locators refer to `sources/ulysses-joyce.txt` by searchable phrase and full-text source line number.

## Story and technique

[T] Bloom walks through Dublin under the name Henry Flower, collects a letter from Martha, considers advertisements and imperial travel, enters a Catholic church, thinks about flowers and sensuality, and walks toward the baths before the funeral. His route is full of delays: he reads packets of tea, watches the post, imagines distant lands, and allows sensation to soften the urgency of the day. The bath at the close is anticipated and imagined rather than narrated as completed.

[T] The prose is a chain of mild interruptions. Commercial copy, liturgical phrases, botanical images, and erotic memory flow into one another. [I] Joyce’s “lotus” is not a single narcotic object; it is the cumulative seduction of postponement, comfort, perfume, and fantasy.

## Important passages and close reading

[T] Bloom’s anticipated bath is both a physical routine and an imagined suspension. [I] The “stream of life” phrase resists a clean division between escape and participation: Bloom imagines floating, but he is also thinking through time, bodies, and the people he will meet. His lethargy is a way of registering the world’s small pressures rather than a total withdrawal from them.

[T] The church scene places bodily desire beside ritual and architecture. [I] Bloom’s outsider curiosity does not become a simple anti-religious argument. He notices choreography, smell, and communal practice as forms of social technology, while the language of salvation is translated into the sensory terms of food, sex, and commerce.

## Homeric echo and crucial difference

[C] In *Odyssey* Book 9, the lotus makes Odysseus’ companions forget home and desire to remain. Bloom’s episode echoes that pull through baths, flowers, advertisements, church ritual, and urban daydreams. [I] The difference is that Bloom never fully forgets home: Molly, Rudy, the funeral, and the unfinished advertisement keep returning. The modern “lotus” is distributed across the city and can coexist with memory.

## Reading question

When Bloom drifts, is he losing the way home, or discovering how much ordinary life depends on small forms of suspension?
