# Episode 18 — “Penelope”

**Scope.** “Penelope” is a title from Joyce’s private schemas, retained here as a research label; interpretive comparisons remain provisional. Locators use `sources/ulysses-18.txt`, with global line numbers cross-referenced by `sources/ulysses-boundaries.json`.

## Story and technique

[T] Molly Bloom lies awake after Bloom returns home. Her long interior monologue moves through illness, household work, lovers, marriage, pregnancy, Gibraltar, music, clothing, memory, and the day’s events. She recalls the afternoon with Blazes Boylan and earlier relationships, judges Bloom with irritation and tenderness, and ends by remembering the proposal and affirming it.

[T] The episode’s extended sentences largely omit punctuation and conventional paragraphing. [I] The form does not create a timeless “female nature.” It gives Molly a continuous verbal field in which bodily sensation, memory, judgment, and desire can coexist without the sorting mechanisms used elsewhere in the novel.

## Important passages and close reading

[T] Molly’s thoughts do not move in a tidy chronology. A household irritation leads to a sexual memory, which leads to Gibraltar, music, a servant, or a political recollection. [I] This is not absence of structure. Repetition, bodily rhythm, and return create a form of attention that refuses the external narrator’s demand for orderly sequence.

[T] The final “yes” follows suspicion, jealousy, memory, and pleasure rather than cancelling them. [I] It can affirm marriage, desire, recollection, or the fact of speech itself. Its power comes from accumulation, not from a simple moral verdict on Molly’s fidelity.

## Homeric echo and crucial difference

[C] “Penelope” recalls the wife who waits in Ithaca and delays the suitors through weaving and unweaving. Molly is likewise placed in the domestic space at the novel’s close, but Joyce gives her an extended interior monologue rather than the outside narration and dialogue through which Homer’s Penelope tests and recognizes Odysseus.

[I] The crucial difference is that Molly’s monologue does not reproduce Homeric chastity or patient strategy. It includes sexuality, resentment, infidelity, work, and affirmation. The echo opens a question about waiting and home; it does not make Molly a modern copy of Penelope.

## Reading question

What does the unpunctuated voice make possible for Molly, and what remains unknowable even after she has spoken so extensively?
