# Motif index for Joyce’s *Ulysses*

This index tracks recurring textual situations across the eighteen episodes and gives each a qualified Homeric touchpoint. It is a reading aid, not a claim that every recurrence has one universal meaning. `[T]` marks a textual recurrence, `[I]` an interpretation, `[C]` context, `[A]` a disputed or qualified reading, `[M]` an analogy, and `[U]` an open question.

## 1. Home, threshold, and return

- **Joyce recurrence:** [1. “Telemachus”](episode-01.md) makes the tower a contested dwelling; [4. “Calypso”](episode-04.md) begins with Bloom inside the domestic routine; [5. “Lotus Eaters”](episode-05.md) lets errands and bath fantasy delay movement; [16. “Eumaeus”](episode-16.md) offers a temporary shelter; [17. “Ithaca”](episode-17.md) brings Bloom to 7 Eccles Street; [18. “Penelope”](episode-18.md) remains in the marital bedroom.
- **Reading:** [T] Doors, shorelines, streets, shelter, house, bed, and garden mark changes in belonging. [I] Home is repeatedly reached as a place and missed as a guarantee. Bloom returns physically, while Stephen remains only provisionally hosted.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [M] Ithaca, Calypso’s island, Eumaeus’ hut, and Penelope’s bed supply distinct models of delay, shelter, recognition, and return. [A] Joyce does not make 7 Eccles Street a restored Homeric palace: no secret identity or purge secures it.
- **Guardrail:** Do not turn “home” into a universal psychological need that overrides the novel’s specific marriage, class, colonial, and urban conditions.

## 2. Fatherhood, sonship, and inheritance

- **Joyce recurrence:** Stephen’s absent or contested father appears in [1](episode-01.md), [2. “Nestor”](episode-02.md), and [3. “Proteus”](episode-03.md); paternity becomes an argument in [9. “Scylla and Charybdis”](episode-09.md); Bloom’s memory of Rudy and his response to Stephen intensify in [4](episode-04.md), [15. “Circe”](episode-15.md), [16](episode-16.md), and [17](episode-17.md).
- **Reading:** [T] The novel presents biological descent, legal fiction, education, care, and imaginative identification as different forms of relation. [I] Bloom can offer hospitality without becoming Stephen’s father, and Stephen can receive help without accepting a completed paternal story.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [C] Telemachus and Odysseus provide the strongest epic father-son frame; [M] Bloom and Stephen briefly occupy a possible modern version of that relation. [A] The analogy remains unfinished by design.
- **Guardrail:** Avoid treating Bloom’s kindness as proof of a healed family or Stephen’s departure as proof that relation failed completely.

## 3. Hospitality, strangers, and exclusion

- **Joyce recurrence:** Deasy’s office in [2](episode-02.md) offers authority without generous hospitality; the funeral carriage in [6. “Hades”](episode-06.md) is a constrained social group; [12. “Cyclops”](episode-12.md) makes the pub both gathering place and exclusionary enclosure; [13. “Nausicaa”](episode-13.md) turns beach encounter into distance; [16](episode-16.md) and [17](episode-17.md) make Bloom’s welcome practical but temporary.
- **Reading:** [T] Food, money, names, seats, shelter, and invitations decide who can remain in a scene. [I] Hospitality in Joyce is material work under pressure, not a moral identity possessed once and for all.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [C] Homer repeatedly tests the relation between host and stranger, from Nestor and Eumaeus to the suitors’ abuse of hospitality. [M] Bloom’s sheltering of Stephen recalls Eumaeus’ care while lacking epic recognition.
- **Guardrail:** Do not call every social encounter “hospitality”; mark when a scene is coercive, transactional, indifferent, or merely shared space.

## 4. Body, food, appetite, and consumption

- **Joyce recurrence:** Bloom’s kidney and household eating in [4](episode-04.md), the bath and sensory drift in [5](episode-05.md), lunch and the hungry city in [8. “Lestrygonians”](episode-08.md), erotic bodies in [13](episode-13.md), birth and medical discourse in [14. “Oxen of the Sun”](episode-14.md), transformations in [15](episode-15.md), and Molly’s bodily memories in [18](episode-18.md) form a dense chain.
- **Reading:** [T] Eating, sex, birth, waste, illness, and fatigue remain adjacent. [I] Joyce makes social power visible through what bodies consume, display, regulate, or refuse; the body is also where care and imagination become concrete.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [M] Lotus, Laestrygonians, Helios’ cattle, and Circe all provide appetite or transformation pressure. [A] The modern episodes do not assign one stable symbol to one bodily act.
- **Guardrail:** Do not equate appetite with moral corruption or purity. Episode 8’s account of Bloom’s Jewish background and dietary choices is specific and mixed, not a general religious rule.

## 5. Language, style, and the making of history

- **Joyce recurrence:** Schoolroom history and Deasy’s rhetoric shape [2](episode-02.md); [7. “Aeolus”](episode-07.md) turns prose into headlines; [9](episode-09.md) turns interpretation into argument; [10. “Wandering Rocks”](episode-10.md) distributes viewpoint; [11. “Sirens”](episode-11.md) makes prose musical; [14](episode-14.md) moves through historical styles; [15](episode-15.md) stages speech as hallucination; [17](episode-17.md) uses catechism; [18](episode-18.md) removes ordinary punctuation.
- **Reading:** [T] Form changes the reader’s access to bodies, memory, public speech, and private thought. [I] The novel’s style experiments test whether inherited language can carry a present life without disguising its exclusions.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [C] Homer’s formula, embedded tales, repeated epithets, and shifts between narration and speech are formal resources rather than a single “style.” [M] Joyce’s episode-by-episode changes answer that inheritance through modern media and parody.
- **Guardrail:** Do not treat the Linati or other schema correspondences as a complete explanation of each episode’s prose. They guide comparison; the text still decides what occurs.

## 6. Religion, ritual, and uncertain belief

- **Joyce recurrence:** Mulligan’s mock Mass and Stephen’s grief appear in [1](episode-01.md); [4](episode-04.md) joins metempsychosis to domestic conversation; [5](episode-05.md) enters a Catholic church; [6](episode-06.md) observes a Christian funeral from an outsider position; [8](episode-08.md), [9](episode-09.md), [14](episode-14.md), [15](episode-15.md), and [18](episode-18.md) rework Christian language, Jewish identity, prayer, birth, death, and affirmation.
- **Reading:** [T] Ritual provides choreography, vocabulary, social membership, and a way to speak about bodies. [I] Joyce’s uncertainty is not a world without ritual; it is a world where ritual can console, exclude, parody, or become administrative language.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [C] Homer’s sacrifices and gods organize the epic’s relations between mortal action and divine order. [M] Joyce’s rituals are mediated by institutions and memory rather than by visible gods.
- **Guardrail:** Do not reduce Jewish, Catholic, Protestant, or secular positions to interchangeable “religion.” Follow the character and scene.

## 7. Nation, empire, and the outsider

- **Joyce recurrence:** English and Italian authority frame [1](episode-01.md); Deasy’s unionist patriotism and antisemitism appear in [2](episode-02.md); press and public memory shape [7](episode-07.md); imperial travel fantasy runs through [4](episode-04.md) and [8](episode-08.md); the viceroy’s route crosses [10](episode-10.md); the Citizen debates belonging with Bloom in [12](episode-12.md); Murphy’s seafaring and Bloom’s homeward movement meet in [16](episode-16.md).
- **Reading:** [T] Names, accents, maps, newspapers, money, and public ceremonies distribute power through Dublin. [I] Bloom’s “nation” argument is one situated civic proposal, not the novel’s final political doctrine; Stephen’s alienation and the Citizen’s violence expose different failures of belonging.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [M] Odysseus returns to a named homeland after war, while Joyce’s characters move through a city whose national status and cultural authority are contested. [A] This is a historical transposition, not a claim that colonial Dublin simply equals Homeric Ithaca.
- **Guardrail:** Keep 1904 Irish colonial history visible. Do not use “empire” as a free-floating metaphor detached from the text’s institutions and speech.

## 8. Death, memory, and the persistence of the absent

- **Joyce recurrence:** Stephen’s dead mother shadows [1](episode-01.md) and [3](episode-03.md); Rudy shapes Bloom’s thought in [4](episode-04.md), the funeral in [6](episode-06.md), song in [11](episode-11.md), and the vision in [15](episode-15.md); Molly’s memories in [18](episode-18.md) keep the past active without arranging it chronologically.
- **Reading:** [T] The dead return as bodies, names, images, phrases, and sudden visions. [I] Memory is neither a stable archive nor a supernatural proof. It interrupts the present and changes what a living person can do next.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [C] Odysseus meets the dead in Book 11 and receives knowledge from them. [M] Joyce makes the underworld domestic and civic: grief circulates through errands, funerals, music, and hallucination rather than producing an oracle.
- **Guardrail:** Do not treat Rudy’s vision or the mother apparition as the same kind of event. The novel changes register from interior association to staged hallucination and leaves their status deliberately unstable.

## 9. Water, sea, travel, and routes

- **Joyce recurrence:** The sea opens [1](episode-01.md) and absorbs Stephen’s thought in [3](episode-03.md); Bloom’s eastern fantasy and city route appear in [4](episode-04.md) and [5](episode-05.md); wind and river imagery shape [7](episode-07.md) and [10](episode-10.md); [16](episode-16.md) fills the shelter with sailor stories; Molly remembers Gibraltar in [18](episode-18.md).
- **Reading:** [T] Water is seen, imagined, crossed, remembered, and turned into language. [I] Routes connect people without guaranteeing arrival: the city’s paths can produce contact, deflection, or exhaustion.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [C] The sea is the material of Odysseus’ wandering and danger. [M] Joyce’s Dublin retains the pressure of travel while making most movement pedestrian, commercial, civic, or conversational.
- **Guardrail:** Avoid turning every river or sea reference into a direct Odyssean voyage. Some are ordinary geography, some are memory, and some are stylistic recurrence.

## 10. Women, agency, and the gaze

- **Joyce recurrence:** Molly’s domestic labor and reading appear in [4](episode-04.md); Martha’s letter and the church scene in [5](episode-05.md); female bodies are filtered through appetite in [8](episode-08.md); Gerty and the beach encounter structure [13](episode-13.md); Mina Purefoy’s labor is the scene around which [14](episode-14.md) male talk proliferates; Bella/Bello drives a transformation in [15](episode-15.md); Molly’s voice occupies [18](episode-18.md).
- **Reading:** [T] Women are imagined, watched, addressed, desired, medically discussed, remembered, and given speech in sharply different forms. [I] Joyce makes the politics of representation part of the event: “Nausicaa” changes style when the presumed object of the gaze changes position, while “Penelope” gives Molly extended speech without making her fully transparent.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [M] Nausicaa, Calypso, Circe, and Penelope are distributed across different Joyce scenes. [A] Molly is not a silent Penelope, Gerty is not simply Nausicaa, and Bella is not merely Circe; the names open comparisons that the scenes also resist.
- **Guardrail:** Do not turn the Homeric women into a single category or treat Joyce’s female voices as automatic emancipation. Track who controls the narration, who is seen, and who bears material consequences.

## 11. Music, voice, and knowledge

- **Joyce recurrence:** Public speech is staged in [1](episode-01.md), classroom and press voices in [2](episode-02.md) and [7](episode-07.md), debate in [9](episode-09.md), song and sound in [11](episode-11.md), theatrical voices in [15](episode-15.md), and Molly’s uninterrupted flow in [18](episode-18.md).
- **Reading:** [T] Voice can carry grief, seduction, authority, ridicule, or memory before its content is settled. [I] “Sirens” is especially resistant to the idea that seduction means only sex: the song’s lure includes knowledge of what has been lost. [A] The balance between erotic and epistemic readings remains a critical question, not a solved fact.
- **Homeric touchpoint:** [C] The Sirens’ song is dangerous because it promises knowledge, while Homer’s bardic performances preserve and reshape memory. [M] Joyce makes the reader hear that problem through typographic and rhythmic prose.
- **Guardrail:** Do not treat sound pattern as a hidden code independent of scene, speaker, and social setting.

## Questions for guided reading

- [U] When does Bloom’s care become a form of hospitality, and when does it become an attempt to write Stephen into a story Stephen has not accepted?
- [A] How does Molly’s extended monologue alter the familiar image of Penelope when Homer’s own Penelope is read as a speaking, testing character?
- [I] Which episode most clearly makes inherited myth answerable to modern history: “Cyclops,” “Oxen of the Sun,” “Circe,” or “Penelope”?
- [U] Which film adaptation will be added once the title, year, and cut are specified?
