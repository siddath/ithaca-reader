# Episode 10 — “Wandering Rocks”

**Scope.** “Wandering Rocks” is a title from Joyce’s private schemas, retained here as a research label; interpretive comparisons remain provisional. Locators refer to `sources/ulysses-joyce.txt` by searchable phrase and full-text source line number.

## Story and technique

[T] The episode presents a series of short Dublin scenes: Father Conmee walks toward Artane; schoolchildren, clerks, sailors, shopkeepers, family members, musicians, and political figures cross the city; the viceroy’s procession moves through the same space. Characters seen in earlier episodes reappear from new angles. No single consciousness controls the whole field.

[T] Joyce uses cuts, reprises, repeated objects, and spatial crossings rather than a continuous protagonist. [I] The “rocks” are not only obstacles. They are the city’s fixed institutions and habits around which people move, each scene becoming a partial account of the same afternoon.

## Important passages and close reading

[T] Father Conmee’s benevolent routine coexists with colonial religion, class hierarchy, and the private histories of people he barely notices. [I] The episode’s irony is structural rather than merely satirical: no one person can see the whole system in which each person moves.

[T] The viceroy’s procession appears grand, but the “brown macintosh” pedestrian crosses it without being absorbed into its meaning. [I] Joyce makes official power one path among many. Public ceremony does not erase the ordinary, the poor, or the accidental.

## Homeric echo and crucial difference

[C] In *Odyssey* Book 12, Circe describes the Wandering Rocks as an alternative sea route; the Argo is said to have passed them, while Odysseus does not sail them. Joyce transfers that indirect hazard from ocean navigation to urban simultaneity: a route is difficult because other lives, institutions, and histories occupy it.

[I] There is no ship and no single navigator. The episode’s distributed form resists reducing Dublin to a disguised voyage. Its Homeric value lies in pressure on movement and obstacle, while its modern difference is a networked city seen through multiple partial viewpoints.

## Reading question

What becomes visible when the city is narrated as a set of intersecting lives rather than as the path of one hero?
