# Book 7 — The palace, the suppliant, and the clothes that tell a story

Primary: [Butler, Book 7](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.7.vii.html), Samuel Butler (1900), [Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1727); exact source `sources/odyssey-07.txt`.

## Story [T]

Nausicaa returns home. Her nurse Eurymedusa, brought from elsewhere as a prize for Alcinous, attends her. Athena hides Odysseus in mist, appears as a young girl carrying a pitcher and guides him to the palace. She explains Arete's standing and advises him to gain her goodwill. He marvels at metalwork, weaving and the garden's continuous abundance, enters unseen, clasps Arete's knees, becomes visible and requests conveyance home. He then sits in the hearth's ashes.

The elder Echeneus prompts Alcinous to seat and feed the stranger. The king promises an escort and wonders whether the visitor might be a god. Odysseus insists on his humanity and hunger. Once others leave, Arete recognises his clothes and asks their origin. He recounts Calypso and his landing, praising Nausicaa. When Alcinous faults his daughter for not escorting him directly, Odysseus takes the responsibility. Alcinous offers the possibility of marriage but explicitly promises not to detain him. He is given a bed.

## Quotations to mark

**B07-Q1 — Echeneus to the king.**

> it is not creditable to you that a stranger should be seen sitting among the ashes of your hearth;

Butler, Book 7, immediately after the appeal. **[I]** A splendid palace does not perform hospitality by itself. Someone must notice the mismatch between material magnificence and the guest's treatment.

**B07-Q2 — Odysseus identifying himself as mortal.**

> I have nothing of the immortal about me, neither in body nor mind,

Butler, Book 7, reply to Alcinous's speculation. **[I]** The hero first asserts a shared condition, not a prestigious name. Hunger and suffering make the claim concrete.

**B07-Q3 — Odysseus on the interruption of grief by hunger.**

> for an empty stomach is a very importunate thing,

Butler, Book 7, same reply. **[I]** Bodily need interrupts even a great sorrow. This is not simply comic deflation: the continuing life of the body requires action before the full story can be told.

**B07-Q4 — Arete examining his clothes.**

> Who, and whence are you, and who gave you those clothes?

Butler, Book 7, private conversation after the feast. **[I]** A material object carries information that a stranger has not yet disclosed. Her practical knowledge becomes an instrument of inquiry.

**B07-Q5 — Alcinous on the proposed stay.**

> but no one (heaven forbid) shall keep you here against your own wish,

Butler, Book 7, marriage offer and escort promise. **[I]** The explicit freedom to depart distinguishes generous reception from the captivity in Book 5.

## Important passages and close reading

**Essential: the mist and the warning about strangers. [T/I]** Athena says the townspeople may be inhospitable, despite the kindness we have seen from Nausicaa. Hospitality is uneven across a community and uncertain before a host takes responsibility. Divine concealment protects the hero, but it also means he does not undergo the normal public scrutiny of an arriving stranger. An entirely idealised reading of Phaeacia loses that complication.

**Essential: Arete's importance. [I/A]** Both Nausicaa and Athena direct him to the queen. Her authority includes the esteem of family and community and a role in settling disputes. This gives a woman striking political significance within the poem; it does not establish a matriarchal society or erase Alcinous's authority. The scene's sequence is revealing: he appeals to Arete, Echeneus prompts the king, the king seats him, and Arete later questions the clothes. Influence is distributed rather than reducible to one visible throne.

**Essential: the palace and garden. [I]** Continuous fruit and remarkable craft contrast with shipwreck and deprivation. Their detailed description suspends the journey in an image of reliable provision. Yet the palace explicitly contains women grinding grain and weaving. Some labour is visible beneath the appearance of effortless plenty. The opening identification of Eurymedusa as a captive prize keeps the same unequal social order in view even in this attractive setting.

**Essential: hearth, ashes and seat. [I]** The movement from the floor to a place beside the ruler stages a change in social reception. It is not Odysseus's body that suddenly becomes human; it is the community's treatment of him that changes. Echeneus's intervention makes the hosts accountable to the standards their own society recognises.

**Essential: Arete's evidence and Odysseus's version. [T/I]** She knows the clothes because she and her household produced them. He gives an account that answers how they reached him, but withholds his celebrated name. His version also protects Nausicaa, shifting the explanation for their separate arrival to his own caution. The reader has the prior scene and can compare narration with event. This is a small, observable exercise in evaluating an interested speaker before the great first-person narrative begins in Book 9.

**[A] Counterreading:** selective narration is not automatically malicious deception. Protecting the person who helped him may be an act of reciprocity. But it also improves his position before a powerful father. Multiple purposes can inhabit the same speech.

**Do not skip: the return he names. [I]** Odysseus longs here for property, bondsmen and the greatness of his house. “Home” includes power and ownership as well as affection. A reading that makes it only a private emotional sanctuary selectively removes part of the hero's expressed desire.

## Mythological context [T/C]

Phaeacian descent is traced to Poseidon; their kinship with gods does not guarantee immunity from him, as Book 13 will show. The palace's immortal guardian dogs are fashioned by Hephaestus, called Vulcan in Butler. Athena's departure to Athens and Erechtheus places the tale within a wider religious geography without making it a documentary record of a single historical period.

Arete is a character's proper name. Avoid building an argument by silently equating it with the differently accented Greek word commonly transliterated *aretē* (“excellence”). English transliteration can conceal distinctions. In this dossier her significance rests on her actions and the descriptions in this book.

## Philosophical stakes, example and Joyce [I/M]

The book tests how much of a person must be known before help is owed, and how help changes the conditions under which a story can be told. It also asks whether ownership of beautiful things demonstrates excellence when a needy person remains in the ashes.

**[M] Example:** a person who made or maintained an object may detect a discrepancy missed by someone in formal authority. Arete's textile knowledge is socially located expertise, not magical omniscience.

There is no compulsory one-episode Joyce equivalent. Bloom's later reception of Stephen provides a thematic comparison in hospitality, but [“Ithaca,” Episode 17](../ulysses/episode-17.md), transforms the setting and scale.
