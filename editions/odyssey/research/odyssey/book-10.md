# Book X — The Bag of Winds, Devouring Harbors, and Circe's House

Primary text: [Samuel Butler, *Odyssey* X](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.10.x.html). Local text: `sources/odyssey-10.txt`. Butler's Jove, Mercury, and Hades correspond to Zeus, Hermes, and the underworld/its ruler.

## What happens, and who knows what

[T] Aeolus entertains Odysseus for a month, listens to the Troy story, seals contrary winds in a hide, and releases the west wind toward Ithaca. After nine days Odysseus sees home and falls asleep. His crew, excluded from knowledge of the bag, imagines private treasure and opens it. The fleet is blown back. Aeolus now infers divine hatred from failure and expels them.

> Thus they talked and evil counsels prevailed. They loosed the sack, whereupon the wind flew howling forth and raised a storm that carried us weeping out to sea and away from our own country.

Odysseus about his crew; Book X, searchable phrase “They loosed the sack”; local lines 46–48.

[T] At Telepylus the Laestrygonians trap every ship moored inside the harbor and destroy them with rocks and spears, eating the captured men. Odysseus' single ship, tied outside, escapes. At Aeaea, Circe drugs a scouting party and turns them into pigs while their human awareness remains. Eurylochus escapes and reports. Hermes equips Odysseus with *moly* and instructions; Odysseus resists the drug, draws his sword, and requires Circe's oath. After sex, he refuses food until she restores his men. They then remain for a year until the crew reminds him of home. Circe directs him to consult Tiresias among the dead.

> They were like pigs—head, hair, and all, and they grunted just as pigs do; but their senses were the same as before, and they remembered everything.

Odysseus about Circe's transformation; Book X, searchable phrase “their senses were the same”; local lines 197–206.

> no man with any sense of what is right can think of either eating or drinking in your house until you have set his friends free

Odysseus to Circe; Book X, searchable phrase “sense of what is right”; local lines 330–334.

> Sir, it is time you began to think about going home

The crew to Odysseus; Book X, searchable phrase “time you began”; local lines 400–405.

## The passages to hold onto

[T] Read the near-home reversal at local lines 32–71 closely. The crew's grievance is not mere curiosity: they believe labor and reward have been distributed unjustly. Odysseus has controlled the rudder and the information for nine days; exhaustion makes him sleep, secrecy makes them suspicious, and their action destroys the common good. The episode is a compact study of failed trust within hierarchy.

[T] The Circe sequence at lines 196–348 matters because it repeatedly asks what remains of a person under domination. The men's minds remain human while their bodies and social standing become livestock. Restoration is not achieved by Odysseus' force alone: divine instruction, a binding oath, a threat, and his refusal of hospitality all matter.

[C] Circe is a divine enchantress in Homer, daughter of the Sun and Perse; later literature enormously expands her biography and symbolism. Hermes' herb *moly* belongs to the Homeric episode, but confident botanical identification is [U]. The Laestrygonians are not simply another Cyclops: their organized city and harbor show that civic structure does not guarantee humane conduct.

## Close readings

[I] The wind-bag episode exposes informational inequality as a political danger. Odysseus tells Aeolus everything but apparently tells his own crew too little. [A] The crew remains responsible for opening a secured divine gift when home is visible. Yet their suspicion grows from a recognizable material disparity: they think the leader has received still more wealth while they return empty-handed. The poem can condemn their act without making command blameless.

[I] Three spaces offer distorted households. Aeolus' family is sealed into luxurious endogamy; Antiphates' household turns arrival into a signal for communal predation; Circe's house turns guests into dependent animals before becoming a place of abundance. Home is not automatically ethical. Ithaca must eventually be judged by how its own household receives strangers and controls appetite.

[I] Circe's sexual relation with Odysseus involves divine power, threat, oath, and negotiation. The text presents Odysseus following Hermes' plan, including accepting her bed only after exacting an oath. [A] Calling the episode either uncomplicated romance or simple conquest loses the asymmetry: she has transformed his men and can harm him; he confronts her with a sword under divine protection. Consent is entangled with leverage on both sides.

[I] Eurylochus repeatedly supplies dissenting knowledge: he waits outside Circe's house, reports the trap, and later urges departure. Odysseus calls resistance cowardice, but the survivor's caution saves the report. [A] Later Eurylochus will lead the cattle transgression, so the poem prevents a stable division between wise leader and wise dissenter.

[I] The full year on Aeaea resembles the lotus in slow motion. There is no drug now forcing forgetfulness, only comfort. The crew must return the narrator of homecoming to his own goal. Leadership circulates.

## Motifs, links, and modern comparison

[T] Links: sleep recurs at decisive failures; feasting alternates between hospitality and predation; human/animal boundaries anticipate Scylla and the cattle of the Sun; the descent instruction opens XI; Circe returns as expert guide in XII.

[M] Modern analogy: the wind-bag resembles a critical system whose administrator keeps sole control and gives the team no usable explanation. Unauthorized opening is still a breach, but “just trust me” creates an avoidable single point of failure. Circe's antidote-and-oath sequence resembles restoring compromised users only after neutralizing the exploit and constraining the operator who caused it.

## Questions

1. [A] Would transparent explanation of the wind-bag have prevented the disaster, or would greed simply have found another pretext?
2. [I] What does continuity of memory inside animal bodies imply about personhood in this episode?
3. [I] Why does the crew, rather than Odysseus, remember home after a year of comfort?

## Provisional Joyce bridge

[M] Joyce's “Aeolus” is organized by rhetorical gusts, interruptions, and newspaper production; the Homeric wind-bag offers a model of controlled forces released by collective misreading. Joyce's “Circe” turns Nighttown into a hallucinatory theater of desire, accusation, and metamorphosis. Neither is a plot transcription: both transpose instability of voice and identity into modern urban media.
