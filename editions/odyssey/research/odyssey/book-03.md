# Book 3 — Nestor, remembered war, and learning to be a guest

Primary reading: [Butler, Book 3](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.3.iii.html). Quotes: Samuel Butler (1900), local `sources/odyssey-03.txt`, [Gutenberg 1727](https://www.gutenberg.org/ebooks/1727). Locate by the exact phrase and scene; English prose lines are not Greek verse numbers.

## Story and knowledge [T]

Telemachus and Athena-as-Mentor reach Pylos during a communal sacrifice to Poseidon. Nestor's son Peisistratus welcomes them into the meal and gives the older stranger precedence. Telemachus is nervous about approaching Nestor, but speaks with Athena's encouragement. Nestor recounts the suffering at Troy, the disagreement among the returning Greeks and his own comparatively direct journey home. His information about Odysseus stops when their paths divide.

Telemachus asks about Agamemnon's murder. Nestor explains Aegisthus's seduction of Clytemnestra, removal of the bard guarding her, the killing, and Orestes's revenge. He recommends seeking Menelaus, who travelled much farther. Athena departs visibly as a bird; Nestor recognises divine favour and promises a sacrifice. The next morning a heifer is offered, Telemachus is bathed and clothed, and Peisistratus drives him towards Sparta. They receive hospitality on the way.

The apparent detour gives Telemachus no immediate reunion. It gives him witnesses, examples, social experience and a new companion. Nestor is authoritative about what he experienced and limited about what happened after separation.

## Quotations to mark

**B03-Q1 — Athena, answering Telemachus's anxiety about speaking.**

> will be suggested to you by your own instinct, and heaven will prompt you further;

Butler, Book 3, Athena's reply before they join the feast. **[I]** Personal ability and divine assistance are presented together. Translating this into “believe in yourself” loses half the scene; translating it into “the god does everything” loses the other half.

**B03-Q2 — Nestor describing the limits of his report.**

> I know neither who got home safely nor who were lost

Butler, Book 3, end of Nestor's own return narrative. **[I]** An experienced speaker marks the boundary between his knowledge and subsequent reports. This is a key reading habit for all the embedded narratives to come.

**B03-Q3 — Athena contrasting survival with Agamemnon's return.**

> provided I could be safe when I was once there.

Butler, Book 3, reply to Telemachus's despair. **[I]** Arrival and successful homecoming are different. The rest of the epic will turn that distinction into action.

**B03-Q4 — Nestor rejecting the idea that his guest should sleep aboard ship.**

> but they will keep open house as I have done.

Butler, Book 3, Nestor's promise concerning his sons. **[I]** Hospitality is a practice transmitted across generations, not simply one generous mood.

## Important passages and interpretation

**Essential: arriving inside a sacrifice. [T/I]** Pylos is introduced through coordinated communal activity. This contrasts with Ithaca's disrupted household and failed assembly. The strangers receive food and ritual participation before they explain who they are. Telemachus now experiences from the guest's side the care he offered Athena in Book 1. Ethical formation occurs through reciprocal roles, not only through advice.

**Essential: Athena praying to Poseidon. [T/I]** The goddess helping Odysseus participates in a prayer to the god opposing him. This prevents a simple “good goddess versus evil god” reading. Divine conflict is specific, while honour and ritual continue within a shared divine world. Poseidon can be a community's honoured god while remaining one traveller's antagonist.

**Essential: Nestor's account of the divided return. [I]** Winning a war does not produce agreement about what comes next. The quarrel begins at an ill-timed gathering with men affected by wine; some leave, others stay, and Odysseus changes groups. Collective action disintegrates once the common military goal ends. This is one reason a post-war poem needs more than a victory scene.

**[A] Counterreading:** Pylos looks stable partly because we see it as a welcomed elite guest. Nestor's family still grieves Antilochus. The successful feast does not mean the household is untouched by the war, nor that its hierarchy is a universal political ideal.

**Essential: the Agamemnon story and the removed bard. [I]** Narrative is not decorative here. Agamemnon leaves a singer to guard his household; Aegisthus removes him before the marriage betrayal succeeds. Compare Phemius under compulsion in Ithaca. A bard's social position can uphold a household's order or be subordinated to those who have seized its space. It is tempting to make the bard “conscience”; that is an interpretive shorthand, whereas the concrete fact is the removal of a person entrusted with a task.

**Do not skip: Nestor's uncertainties. [I]** Telemachus begins by asking for information even if it is painful. Nestor cannot provide an answer to everything. Length and confidence of speech are not the same as universal knowledge. Compare Menelaus's report of Proteus in Book 4 and Odysseus's first-person adventures in Books 9–12: information changes medium and evidential status.

**Do not skip: the heifer's gilded horns, skilled work and shared portions. [I]** The ritual makes visible an ordered division of tasks: goldsmith, sons, women, attendants and guest. Honour is embodied in material preparation. This ordered meal contrasts with the cattle offence in Book 12, where hungry men imitate sacrificial procedures while lacking permission to kill the animals.

## Mythological and social context [T/C]

Nestor is an older survivor and ruler of Pylos, associated here with his father Neleus and his own sons. Agamemnon and Menelaus are the sons of Atreus. Clytemnestra is Agamemnon's wife; Orestes their son; Aegisthus is her partner and Agamemnon's killer in the account. Keep this household as a warning parallel, not a substitute cast list for Ithaca.

The later tragic elaborations of Orestes's matricide, pursuit by the Erinyes and trial belong to other works. This book mentions funeral rites for his mother but does not stage the later tragic sequence. Likewise Nestor's account of Athena's anger does not narrate every offence at Troy known from later mythography. Silence is not permission to insert an entire later story as if it appeared here.

Working Greek ideas remain *xenia*, *nostos* and *kleos*: received hospitality, the vulnerable return, and fame reproduced in exemplary accounts. [Nagy's Homeric commentary](https://www-current.chs.harvard.edu/curated-article/gregory-nagy-a-sampling-of-comments-on-the-iliad-and-odyssey/) offers book-and-passage discussions for further philological inquiry; these interpretations are scene-based rather than a claim of scholarly unanimity.

## Philosophical stakes [I]

**Education by participation:** a hesitant speaker learns through being drawn into a practice. **The limits of expertise:** age brings testimony but does not erase the limits of location and experience. **Home as a relation, not a coordinate:** safety, reception and continuity matter more than merely reaching the correct shore. **The ethical force of examples:** Orestes may inspire courage, but imitation without attention to changed circumstances would be dangerous.

**[M] Modern analogy:** visiting a respected older colleague after a family crisis can yield useful testimony and a model of conduct without yielding the missing answer. The lesson is to receive both the help and its limits.

## Joyce comparison and questions [M]

Joyce names his second episode “Nestor” in the traditional schema, but its schoolmaster Deasy is no straightforward embodiment of wisdom. The comparison lets Joyce test what age and institutional authority deserve. See [Episode 2](../ulysses/episode-02.md).

1. How does being hosted alter Telemachus's understanding of his own household?
2. Which parts of Nestor's story are eyewitness knowledge, and which are reports?
3. When does an example help someone act, and when does it tempt them to force the present into an old pattern?
