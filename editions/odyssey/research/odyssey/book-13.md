# Book XIII — Ithaca Seen Through Mist: Return Becomes Disguise

Primary text: [Samuel Butler, *Odyssey* XIII](https://classics.mit.edu/Homer/odyssey.13.xiii.html). Local text: `sources/odyssey-13.txt`. Butler's Minerva, Jove, and Neptune are Athena, Zeus, and Poseidon.

## What happens, and who knows what

[T] The Phaeacians load Odysseus' gifts, carry him asleep to Ithaca, and place him and the treasure on shore. Poseidon complains that their successful escort has diminished his honor. With Zeus' permission he petrifies the returning ship; the Phaeacians recall an old prophecy and sacrifice in fear. The text leaves their future escort practice under threat.

[T] Athena wraps Ithaca in mist. Odysseus wakes, does not recognize home, counts his goods, and suspects his hosts cheated him. Athena approaches disguised as a shepherd. Even after hearing he is in Ithaca, Odysseus spontaneously invents a Cretan identity and homicide story. Athena reveals herself, praises his craft, identifies the landscape, helps hide the treasure, and plans revenge. She disguises him as an old beggar and sends him to Eumaeus while she retrieves Telemachus.

## The passages to hold onto

[T] The waking and inventory (local lines 174–223) convert fulfilled return into epistemic uncertainty. Odysseus is physically home, but without recognition he cannot inhabit home as home. His first acts are suspicion and accounting. The Athena exchange (221–308) then makes deceptive speech a shared craft, while the revealed harbor, olive, cave, and Neritum restore place through named landmarks.

[C] The Phaeacians occupy a liminal position between ordinary mortals and gods; their ships cross space with uncanny ease. Poseidon's punishment enforces divine honor against a community whose hospitality enables the return he opposes. This is not a modern legal judgment with clear proportionality. The episode asks what a smaller community pays when caught between a guest's need and a god's grievance.

## Close readings

[I] Return is not a coordinate but a recognition. Athena's mist externalizes a real problem without making her merely psychological: the goddess actively conceals the land. Odysseus' inability to recognize Ithaca parallels Ithaca's coming inability to recognize him. Only a sequence of signs, stories, and tests can turn location into restored relationship.

[I] Odysseus lies before necessity requires it. Athena delights in the performance because she shares *metis*, adaptive intelligence. [A] The scene can celebrate matched minds. It can also make the reader uneasy: habitual survival craft persists when danger is not yet established. Can a self trained by twenty years of war and wandering simply “drop” strategic identity?

[I] The Phaeacians demonstrate collective exposure. They fulfill a promise and suffer Poseidon's retaliation after Odysseus has already benefited. [A] Within heroic-divine honor, the god answers a perceived loss of status. From the community's perspective, an innocent crew and future guests bear the cost of conflict between powerful males.

[I] Athena's revenge plan depends on controlled information and unequal recognition. She and Odysseus know more than every Ithacan; Telemachus will be admitted next, while Penelope, Laertes, servants, and suitors receive different tests. Murnaghan argues that private recognitions recruit the human accomplices needed for the apparently epiphanic victory. Disguise is therefore a political allocation of knowledge, not costume alone.

[I] The treasure is hidden before family is contacted. Material security, tactical secrecy, and relationship are ordered deliberately. [A] This looks prudent in an occupied household, yet Athena herself notices that another returnee might rush to spouse and child. The hero's caution protects home while postponing intimacy.

## Motifs, links, and modern comparison

[T] Links: sleep again accompanies a decisive transition, now successfully; the olive and cave foreshadow the rooted marriage-bed in XXIII; Cretan tales recur in XIV and XIX; bodily disguise structures XVII–XXII; landscape recognition culminates with Laertes' trees in XXIV.

[M] Modern analogy: arriving in a familiar account after a long lockout but finding the interface altered. Possession has technically been restored, yet identity must be re-established through trusted markers, recovery secrets, and staged access. Odysseus' hidden treasure functions like quarantining valuable data before testing a compromised system.

## Questions

1. [I] Why must Odysseus fail to recognize Ithaca before Ithaca fails to recognize him?
2. [A] Is Athena's delight in his lie evidence of admirable affinity or a warning about ingrained deception?
3. [I] What obligation did the Phaeacians have to their guest once Poseidon's hostility was known?

## Provisional Joyce bridge

[M] “Return” is the governing problem of Joyce's later episodes, but no single chapter maps neatly onto XIII. A productive bridge is the estrangement of the familiar city: one can be geographically home while identity, household standing, and perception remain unsettled.
