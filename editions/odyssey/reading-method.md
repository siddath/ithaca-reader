# Turning this research into a reading together

The research archive contains the ending and cross-book spoilers. A guided session can use only one book at a time. The default sequence is all 24 Odyssey books in their narrated order, followed by a sustained Joyce reading if that is the intended comparison. We can bring in individual Joyce echoes after the Homeric scene is clear; no prior mastery of Joyce is needed for Homer.

## The rhythm of a session

1. **Read the primary book.** Use the online link or saved source. Before consulting interpretation, identify what happens, who tells us, and what the participants do not know.
2. **Revisit a few important passages.** The dossiers mark quoted words and explain their scene. Read the surrounding paragraph too: a slogan can reverse its meaning when removed from the person speaking it.
3. **Explain the myth when it becomes relevant.** Keep a small cast of names in view. When an allusion requires an older or later text, name that source before connecting it to Homer.
4. **Try the interpretation against the evidence.** Trace the sequence from concrete action or image to a proposed meaning. Then read the counterargument. A compelling metaphor is an invitation to test, not a fact established by fluent wording.
5. **Return to the human stakes.** Who can choose, refuse, leave, speak or punish? What does the household's survival mean for family, guests, dependents and enemies?
6. **Make a comparison.** Distinguish “Joyce deliberately invokes this episode” from “these scenes can productively be read together.” The latter can be valuable without claiming direct equivalence.
7. **Leave one question open.** The reading should increase attention, not force a definitive moral for every book.

There is no required reading speed or invented deadline. We can spend several sessions on Books 9, 11, 19, 22 or 23 if their problems demand it. Less famous transitions still deserve attention: without the hospitality of Book 14 or Eumaeus's history in Book 15, the climax loses much of its human structure.

## A simple annotation key

| Mark | Use |
|---|---|
| **PASSAGE** | Words worth returning to, with their speaker and scene |
| **IMAGE** | A simile, repeated object or pattern; distinguish explicit comparison from our symbolic inference |
| **MYTH** | A god, genealogy, ritual or older story requiring context |
| **QUESTION** | Something the text leaves unresolved or a disagreement among interpretations |
| **POWER** | A difference in status, freedom, vulnerability or consequences |
| **ECHO** | A recurrence within Homer or a qualified comparison with Joyce |

The dossier's [T/C/I/A/M/U] labels tell you the status of a statement. These reading marks tell you what to pay attention to. They serve different purposes.

## Where our first conversation begins

Start with [Book 1](research/odyssey/book-01.md). The central question is why an epic named for Odysseus makes us first live among those waiting for him. Read the divine council, the welcome to Mentes, the quarrel over Phemius's song, and Eurycleia's introduction. Those four scenes establish responsibility, hospitality, authority over stories, and the unequal household that the rest of the poem will test.
